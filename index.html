Level 017 · Why Architecture?

An organisation has to keep going.

It has to honour the commitments already made

and find ways to take on the work still to come.

How can it keep its capacity to continue while taking responsibility for what its way of continuing makes possible for others?

Customers rely on promises. Employees plan their lives around its continuing.

Suppliers, investors and communities each meet its activity on their own terms.

Level 017 · Responsibility in Practice

Some arrangements are built on purpose:

mandates, processes, resources.

Others grow through habit, conversation, feeling,

and expectations nobody ever writes down.

You can hold a responsibility and still be unable to exercise it.

No information. No time. No access to the person whose help you need.

Seeing the difficulty before you can explain why it matters.

Level 018 · An Inquiry through Cybernetics and Complexity
Cybernetics

How people recognise what is happening, coordinate what they do, and use knowledge of consequences to shape what happens next.

And how our own ways of observing shape what reaches judgement.

Complexity

How people’s intentions meet in relationships whose development no participant can fully determine.

A response can sustain a familiar pattern, amplify a small departure, or open what no one foresaw.

The architecture gives these activities some durability.

The Dynamics of Fluidity show how their relationships keep forming.

Level 018 · Memory and Continuing Formation

Records keep the agreements.

People keep what helped, what hurt,

and why a course was chosen.

Passed on, that know-how lets successors reconsider with some sense of the history.

Lost, it leaves them meeting an old difficulty alone.

An expectation can acquire the force of an obligation.

A commitment endures for years while the means of honouring it change.

Level 019 · Carrying Responsibility Forward

Level 046 · What the Law Asks of Companies and Directors

A company owes obligations in its own name.

Companies Act 2006 · s.172(1) · having regard to

  2. the interests of the company’s employees
  3. relationships with suppliers, customers and others
  4. the impact on the community and the environment
  5. a reputation for high standards of business conduct

Level 132 · Recognising Responsibility

The definition given at the top of the shaft,

now carrying everything learnt on the way down.

A responsible organisation
  1. pursues its purposes and sustains its commercial viability
  2. meets its legal obligations
  3. takes moral responsibility for how its activity affects others
  4. builds the relationships, authority, knowledge and resources through which people can judge, question, learn and respond
  5. and gives these commitments practical meaning through its culture and collective conduct

Each generation receives something it did not create,

finds out more of what it means, and adds to what others will inherit.

Consequences can inform further action

when people have ways to recognise, communicate and examine them.

Those responses change the circumstances in which others act.

Responsibility continues through this involvement, including the willingness to reconsider how the organisation understands its own activity.
